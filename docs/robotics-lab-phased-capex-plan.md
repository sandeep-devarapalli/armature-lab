# Robotics Lab: Phased Capex Plan

**Open lean, expand from revenue**

*Companion to the financial model. The logic: spend the minimum to open a lab that can genuinely earn, prove demand, then fund the expensive expansion out of operating cash instead of raising more capital for equipment you cannot yet keep busy. Estimates, not quotes. Not financial advice.*

---

## Why phase it

The full lab is ~₹75 lakh. Committing all of that before a single tenant has signed is the biggest avoidable risk in this business. Phasing does three things:

- **Cuts the amount at risk at launch** from ~₹75 lakh to ~₹50 lakh.
- **Ties the expensive kit to proven demand.** You buy the second workstation, the CNC, and the AMR only once the first ones are booked out.
- **Turns capex into a signal.** Every Phase 2 purchase is triggered by a real number (utilization, tenants, waitlist), not a hunch.

The one thing you cannot phase is the fit-out. Three-phase wiring, clean circuits, HVAC, extraction, cameras, and fire safety all get done at launch, because retrofitting them into a running lab is disruptive and more expensive.

Everything needed to open the doors, run the core formats, and sell all three membership tiers including a real robotic-arm bay.

| Category | What's in | Cost |

| 3D printing | FDM ×3, resin printer + wash/cure, starting stock | ₹3.5 L |
| Machine shop | Drill press, grinder, vises, hand and power tools | ₹1.0 L |

| Sensors | Depth cameras ×2, 2D LiDAR ×2, IMUs and basic sensors | ₹1.8 L |
| Robotics | One collaborative arm, drone cage, one test drone | ₹10.0 L |
| Infrastructure and fit-out | Full electrical (5 boards, 3-phase, UPS), HVAC and extraction, 9 cameras + NVR, access control, fire safety, furniture and 16 pod desks, demo AV, PPE | ₹14.5 L |
| **Phase 1 total** | | **~₹50.0 L** |

**What you can already sell on Phase 1:** individual and serious memberships in full, all sixteen dedicated builder pods, the robotic-arm bay, drone-cage slots, prototyping and electronics stations, DGX and GPU time, and at least one or two company tenancies. This is a complete, credible lab, not a stripped one. At 3,500 sq ft the fit-out line is tight: hold ₹1.5 to 2 lakh of contingency for the longer electrical and HVAC runs and the pod furniture.

## Phase 2: Full build-out (funded from revenue)

The additions that turn a working lab into the full facility, bought once the Phase 1 kit is busy. This is the layer you expand into, not launch with.

| Category | What gets added | Cost | Why it waits |
|---|---|---|---|
| Compute and AI | Second RTX workstation | ₹3.5 L | Add when training and CAD demand outgrows one machine |
| 3D printing | Large-format / industrial FDM, laser cutter | ₹4.5 L | Add when print queue backs up and enclosure work is constant |
| Machine shop | Benchtop CNC mill/router, upgraded dust extraction | ₹2.5 L | Add when fabrication is a real bottleneck, not occasional |
| Electronics and PCB | Reflow oven + stencil + manual pick-and-place, second oscilloscope, more component stock | ₹2.6 L | Add when in-house PCB assembly volume justifies it |
| Sensors | 3D LiDAR, two more depth cameras, RTK GPS, force/torque sensor | ₹3.3 L | Add when perception and drone projects need it |
| Robotics | Two educational arms, one mobile robot / AMR, more drones and spares | ₹7.5 L | Add when the single arm is booked out and AMR demand appears |
| Infrastructure | More lockers and secure storage, second demo AV, expanded access control | ₹1.0 L | Add as tenant count grows |
| **Phase 2 total** | | **~₹24.9 L** | |
| **Cumulative after Phase 2** | | **~₹74.9 L** | |

The heaviest Phase 2 line is robotics (a second and third arm plus an AMR), which is exactly the kit that should follow proven arm-bay demand rather than lead it.

## Phase 3: Scale (optional, demand-driven)

Only if the lab is consistently profitable and contended. Not budgeted here, listed so you know where the next rupee goes.

- Second DGX Spark and additional GPU capacity, ~₹10 to 12 L, when compute is fully booked.
- A second serious collaborative arm, ~₹7 L, when one arm cannot meet tenant demand.
- Dedicated, built-out company bays and a second or larger drone cage, ~₹5 to 8 L.

Indicative Phase 3: **₹20 to 27 L**, entirely funded from profit.

---

## The triggers

Do not move to the next phase on a calendar. Move on these signals.

| Move | Trigger |
|---|---|
| Phase 1 → Phase 2 | Core equipment (arm, printers, benches) running above 60 to 70 percent utilization, a booking waitlist forming, **2 to 3 company tenants signed**, and the lab at or near cash break-even (~₹7.65 L/month) |
| Within Phase 2 | Buy each item only when its specific bottleneck is real: CNC when fabrication backs up, second arm when the first is booked out, AMR when a tenant needs mobile robotics |
| Phase 2 → Phase 3 | Consistent monthly profit, 4 or more company tenants, DGX and arm fully booked, and turning away demand |

## Capex and funding summary

| Phase | Capex | Cumulative | Funded by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Phase 1 (launch) | ~₹50 L | ~₹50 L | Raised capital / founder capital |
| Phase 2 (build-out) | ~₹25 L | ~₹75 L | Operating cash, once break-even is in sight |
| Phase 3 (scale) | ~₹20 to 27 L | ~₹95 to 102 L | Profit |

**The plan in one line:** raise for a ~₹50 lakh launch plus 6 to 9 months of runway, get to 2 to 3 company tenants and 60 percent-plus utilization, then let the lab buy its own Phase 2. That keeps the capital you raise tied to the lab you can actually fill.
